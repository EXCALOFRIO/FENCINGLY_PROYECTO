# FENCINGLY

### What we extract from a fencing clip, what each piece is worth, and the videos that show it

We are trying to learn **who has right of way** in a foil action. Foil is a weapon of *convention*:
the valid touch is not simply the first one to land, so the answer **cannot be read off the scoring
lights**. That is the whole reason this project exists - and it is the one thing that does not work
yet: our detector scores **69.7 %** against **73.8 % for never answering at all**, so it ships
**off**. It is in the capability table below with that number next to it, and this page is about
everything that *is* extracted along the way.

Everything below is **generated by us** from the clips: skeletons, masks, normals, alphas, threat
cones, piste maps. Every video and every figure on this page comes out of the pipeline - {{N_VIDEOS}}
videos in all - and each one carries **its measured error printed on the frame**, not just its name.

## Where each capability stands

Several of these are published as **not working**. That is deliberate: a field without its error is a
promise, and a capability without its failures is a brochure.

| capability | state |
|---|---|
| both fencers tracked, no intruders | **works** - median 100 % of frames, worst clip 91 % |
| always two poses, invented ones marked | **works** - 0 delivered frames without both |
| provenance of every point | **works** - and it is the part that cannot be recovered later |
| the five model channels | **works** - referee leakage 0.0000 |
| movement events | **works** - 90 % recall, `march -> lunge` confusion = 0 |
| weapon hand | **works on a held-out set** - the cascade 1.000 over 19 of 19; its front-foot rung alone covers 12 of those 19 |
| piste rectified from its two edges | **works** - 20 of 22 clips |
| **origin** on the piste, measured not assumed | **works where the colour frontiers are visible** - 13 of 22 clips, held-out landmark to **p50 5 cm** |
| scoring lights | **works** - 90.5 % with a per-venue region of interest |
| foil blade and threat cone | **partial** - the blade is found in **42.6 %** of 1080p crops but **1.6 %** at 720p |
| position **across** the piste | **partial** - metres between detected edges, but the physical control passes in only 3 of 22 clips |
| **scale along** the piste | **does NOT work** - three independent routes, none passes its own control, and there is a geometric reason |
| referee told apart from a fencer | **does NOT work** - ~6 % of clips, four routes failed |
| **right of way** (the goal) | **does NOT work** - 69.7 % against **73.8 % for never answering**, and beaten on both halves of the job |
| parry | **not implemented** - 0 annotated, blade contact not verifiable by eye in this material |
| depth from `pointmap` | **discarded with measurements** - the free `foot y` orders depth better |

### Parry - why there is nothing here

**0 parries annotated**, because blade contact turned out **not verifiable by eye** in this material:
of 16 blind plates, **zero** were decidable. Without ground truth there is nothing to measure
against, so there is nothing to ship. `halt` is **not** a parry: it means the fencer stopped moving.

## This table **is** the training input

Everything above is the model's input. Not the video: **the series**.

Per clip and per fencer the model gets the 308-point pose with its provenance, the five channels, the
position along the piste where there is a scale, and the movement events as a row each - with their
type, their index, their start and end frame, their magnitude in metres, and the flags that say how
far each one can be trusted. The label to learn is **right of way**.

Which is why every row carries its **precision** and not just its name. Several are published as
**not usable yet**, and one - the referee - as a **known defect with a flag**, because a model trained
on data that hides its own defects learns the defects.

```python
ev.query("side == 1 and type == 'march' and index_of_type == 3")
```

returns the third march of the right-hand fencer: start frame, end frame, second, metres covered, how
long it took, and how much of it was measured rather than filled in.

## Honest limits

Figures come from **19 to 60 clips** depending on the measurement, **not** from the full corpus.

Ground truth for the weapon hand, for step counting and for blade contact **was built by eye inside
this project**, not by an FIE referee, and is kept in files so it can be contradicted.

Where a number appears twice on this page with two different denominators, that is deliberate: some
controls count **clips**, some count **fencers** and some count **frames**, and pooling them would
invent a figure that was never measured.

Four results in this project fell apart when the sample grew or when a control was finally written -
15/15 became 22/23, a feature changed sign on a bigger set, a blade dispersion went from 14.0 deg to
4.2 deg, and a **published accuracy turned out to belong to the whole cascade and not to the rule it
was credited to**. That is why criteria here are declared before measuring, samples are broken down
by bout and by venue before being aggregated, and **accuracy and coverage are never merged into one
sentence**.

## Fencing terms

| term | meaning |
|---|---|
| **march** / **retreat** | a step forward / a step backward |
| **lunge** | the explosive attack: the front foot flies and **the rear foot stays planted**. That binary property, not the distance, is what separates it from a march |
| **halt** | the fencer stops moving. **This is not a parry**, and the parry is not implemented |
| **right of way** | which fencer has priority for the touch |
| **valid target** | in foil, the torso. A **white light** means the tip landed **off-target** |
| **on-guard** | the basic stance; the **front foot is on the same side as the weapon arm** |
| **lame** | the conductive jacket that makes a valid touch register |
