# FENCINGLY

### What we extract from a fencing clip, what each piece is worth, and the videos that show it

We are trying to learn **who has right of way** in a foil action. Foil is a weapon of *convention*:
the valid touch is not simply the first one to land, so the answer **cannot be read off the scoring
lights**. That is the whole reason this project exists - and it is the one thing that does not work
yet: our detector scores **69.7 %** against **73.8 % for never answering at all**, so it ships
**off**. It is in the capability table below with that number next to it, and this page is about
everything that *is* extracted along the way.

Everything below is **generated by us** from the clips: skeletons, masks, normals, alphas, threat
cones, piste maps. Every video and every figure on this page comes out of the pipeline - 38
videos in all - and each one carries **its measured error printed on the frame**, not just its name.

### How a pose gets found: four rounds, and what each one recovers

A pose is not simply *detected or not*. There are four rounds, and **each one is counted separately** so you can see what would be missing without it.

**The pose is Sapiens 2 in every round.** What changes between rounds is **where the box comes from**: YOLO gives only the box, and the 308 points are always measured by the same pose head. Round 2 exists because that head is **top-down** - it needs no detector to confirm the person, only a box - so it can try exactly where the detector already failed twice. Round 3 is the only one with no measurement left in it.

![cascada_deteccion](media/cascada_deteccion.png)

| round | where the **box** comes from (the pose is always Sapiens 2) | recovers | of 21,950 fencer-frames |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| **0** | YOLO on the whole frame | 21,274 | 96.92 % |
| **1** | **YOLO again, on a crop** - the redetection | 252 | 1.15 % |
| **2** | **interpolated box, no detector at all** | 50 | 0.23 % |
| **3** | no box and no pose: **the pose itself is interpolated** | 600 | 2.73 % |

Read it as a cascade: **without round 1 the pose would be missing in 3.08 %** of fencer-frames. Round 1 - the redetection - recovers **252 of them**, leaving **2.96 %** with no measured pose. Round 2 recovers **50 more**, and what is left, **2.73 %**, is interpolated and **carries a flag saying so**.

Round 2 is only attempted where the gap is **24 frames or shorter** (50 of 295 cases). Beyond that it is not a tuning problem: the quality gate passes **0.6 %** of the time with a fencer in the box and **12.7 %** on an empty patch of background, so it does not discriminate at all - the fencer is simply not in those frames.

**A redetection you can look at.** Left: the full frame, with the box where the detector found nobody. Right: the same box as a crop, where the pose came out.

### 6 - FOIL: the threat cone, as a graded probability field

The cone is what turns *where the arm points* into *who can be hit*. It is **not** a flat wedge: the fill **is the weight function the measurement itself uses**, so the figure and the number cannot disagree. This project already lost a day to a visualisation that did not match its own data, so the drawing and the metric are now the same code.

The yellow cross is where **the two cones cross each other**. That is the test - cone against cone, not cone against torso.

- reach **1.10 m** wrist-to-tip. A foil blade is **0.90 m** on its own, and the first version used exactly that - which is wrong for a *threat* radius, because the threat starts at the wrist and the guard and the hand add the rest. It looked too short and it was
- the axis is the **forearm**, `elbow -> hand root`. Using the hand axis instead put the cone more than 40 deg off in **63.6 %** of frames; the forearm brings that to **15.7 %**
- the arcs are **measured, not chosen**: solid = p50 core **+-14.4 deg half-angle (28.8 deg full)**, dashed = p90 envelope **+-33.0 deg half-angle (66.0 deg full)**, both from |detected blade - forearm axis| over 31 blades. Every angle here is written as *half and full*, on purpose: that undeclared convention already cost this project five contradictory figures in one day. Those two arcs are the pair for an **assumed** direction; where the blade was measured there is only one arc, at +-8.6 deg, because there is no measured p50 for that provenance and inventing one would be a third figure
- when a blade is actually **detected** the field is **centred on the measured blade itself** (`origen 0`) and narrows to **+-8.6 deg**, which is **3.8x** narrower than the +-33.0 deg it uses when the direction is only assumed. Centre and width are **one decision**, not two: the 33.0 is by definition the p90 of |blade - forearm|, so the moment the centre moves onto the blade that percentile no longer applies, and 8.6 is the p90 of what is left over
- **two figures on this page were wrong and are retired with their measurements**, because that is the only way a correction is worth anything. The **layer videos used to centre the cone on the forearm while the caption said it was centred on the measured blade** - in the frame a reader pointed at, the blade sat **21.4 deg** off the axis, outside the solid p50 core, and it was not a rare frame: **26 of 60**. And the width for a seen blade was published as **4.2 deg**, which is the frame-to-frame tremor of the blade's *direction* (70 pairs over 12 bouts, itself a correction of an earlier 14.0 from 8 pairs of one bout) - a real number, but **not** the width of a wedge whose apex is the hand: the detected blade line misses the hand root by 7 px median at only 0.24 of the reach, and a few pixels that close to the apex subtend many degrees. At 4.2 deg the cone **did not contain the blade it was centred on** in 21 of 60 frames. `temblor_hoja_gr` still ships, as its own field, because it measures something real - just not this
- **honest limit, broken down instead of pooled**: a blade line is found in **42.6 %** of crops at 1920x1080 and in **1.6 %** at 1280x720 - and **81 %** of the corpus is 720p. So most of the time the cone is a **geometric prior, not a detection**, and a single pooled percentage would hide which clips it is true for
- the thumb axis was tried as the blade direction and **failed**: lever arm of 2.4-4.8 px, jumps over 40 deg in 10-26 % of frames, against 0.9-2.1 deg for the forearm

### The movement events

March, retreat, lunge, halt, arm extension - one row per event, not per frame. This is the low-dimensional series the model is meant to learn from.

- **90 % recall**, and the confusion that mattered, **march mistaken for lunge, is 0**
- the discriminator is the **rear foot**: in a lunge the front foot flies and the rear foot **stays planted**. That binary property, not the distance, is what separates the two, and the ratio is measured **in the extension phase** and not over the whole window
- overlaps between different event types went from **73 to 0**, enforced by an assertion
- detection lag **+0.5 frames (+17 ms)** over 62 pairings, with no bias
- `balestra` and `jump` ship with **0 events**. The balestra was killed by ballistics: the 8.5 cm of rise it needs would require **7.9 frames of flight**, and the candidate events lasted one frame


### Which hand holds the weapon

What decides is a **cascade**, and its first rung is the rule that carries it: **in the on-guard stance the front foot is on the same side as the weapon arm** (FIE t.115.1). It is counter-intuitive and it is what works. If the front-foot vote is not firm, an on-guard-and-in-action gate takes over; what neither settles is flagged `uncertain`.

![weapon hand](media/weapon_hand.png)

- **the cascade: 1.000 accuracy over 19 of 19 fencers** of a held-out set that played no part in choosing the rule, and **0.949 over all 59** fencers with ground truth
- **the front-foot rule on its own**, with a firm vote: **12 of 12 with no failure, covering 12 of those 19**. Ungated it scores **0.895 over 19** and 0.881 over 59. The 1.000 belongs to the cascade and the coverage to the rule, and they are not the same claim: an earlier version of this page merged them into one sentence
- the previous rule, *the hand closest to the opponent*, scored **0.831 over 59** - wrong about one time in six, and 0.741 on the first 27 annotated
- the glove was tried and **failed**: the whiteness feature reads the **sleeve** (pearson 0.681), and dark gloves exist. Paired separation **-0.002**, i.e. backwards
- what cannot be decided is flagged `uncertain` rather than guessed


### The open defect: sometimes one of the two fencers is the referee

In about **6 % of clips the pipeline delivers the REFEREE as one of the two fencers.** Four detection routes were tried and **all four failed, each with a number**:

| route | why it failed |
|---|---|
| uniform whiteness | the gold **lame** fires it on correct pairs; paired separation came out at **-0.002**, i.e. backwards |
| mask held in hand | with a loose threshold it becomes a darkness detector: fires on 469 of 3,386 patches while only 1 of 39 fencers holds a mask |
| geometry | the referee passed all three geometric gates **without touching them** (separation 1.93 of 3.20, foot delta 0.46 of 0.55, feet-in-band 0.894 of 0.40) - and it was predictable, because a referee walks beside the piste |
| movement behaviour | the referee scored **0.524** events per second, **above the p10 of real fencers (0.493)** and four times its own opponent's rate: walking arm-swing fires the arm-extension event |

Every row carries a `side_valid` flag and it is a **lower bound**. The reason this is hard is worth stating: **the referee's pose is perfectly good.** There is nothing wrong with the pose; what is wrong is *whose* it is. No pose-quality metric will ever catch it.

**0 parries annotated**, because blade contact turned out **not verifiable by eye** in this material:

against, so there is nothing to ship. `halt` is **not** a parry: it means the fencer stopped moving.

## This table **is** the training input

Everything above is the model's input. Not the video: **the series**.

Per clip and per fencer the model gets the 308-point pose with its provenance, the five channels, the
position along the piste where there is a scale, and the movement events as a row each - with their
type, their index, their start and end frame, their magnitude in metres, and the flags that say how
far each one can be trusted. The label to learn is **right of way**.

Which is why every row carries its **precision** and not just its name. Several are published as
**not usable yet**, and one - the referee - as a **known defect with a flag**, because a model trained
on data that hides its own defects learns the defects.

```python
ev.query("side == 1 and type == 'march' and index_of_type == 3")
```

returns the third march of the right-hand fencer: start frame, end frame, second, metres covered, how
long it took, and how much of it was measured rather than filled in.

## Honest limits

Figures come from **19 to 60 clips** depending on the measurement, **not** from the full corpus.

Ground truth for the weapon hand, for step counting and for blade contact **was built by eye inside
this project**, not by an FIE referee, and is kept in files so it can be contradicted.

Where a number appears twice on this page with two different denominators, that is deliberate: some
controls count **clips**, some count **fencers** and some count **frames**, and pooling them would
invent a figure that was never measured.

Four results in this project fell apart when the sample grew or when a control was finally written -
15/15 became 22/23, a feature changed sign on a bigger set, a blade dispersion went from 14.0 deg to
4.2 deg, and a **published accuracy turned out to belong to the whole cascade and not to the rule it
was credited to**. That is why criteria here are declared before measuring, samples are broken down
by bout and by venue before being aggregated, and **accuracy and coverage are never merged into one
sentence**.

## Fencing terms

| term | meaning |
|---|---|
| **march** / **retreat** | a step forward / a step backward |
| **lunge** | the explosive attack: the front foot flies and **the rear foot stays planted**. That binary property, not the distance, is what separates it from a march |
| **halt** | the fencer stops moving. **This is not a parry**, and the parry is not implemented |
| **right of way** | which fencer has priority for the touch |
| **valid target** | in foil, the torso. A **white light** means the tip landed **off-target** |
| **on-guard** | the basic stance; the **front foot is on the same side as the weapon arm** |
| **lame** | the conductive jacket that makes a valid touch register |
